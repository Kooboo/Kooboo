# Hub Page Composition Families

These five families were distilled from 29 of the 88 reviewed template sites. Choose one primary family for a directory, discovery, event, or resource Page. Preserve UUPM topology and real capability boundaries.

## Hub 1 — Search is the homepage

Choose this when users arrive with a clear discovery goal such as a market, restaurant, surf spot, or city activity. Move directly from a compact header into search, filters, or condition selection, then show real preview results for the current condition in the same viewport or immediately below. Organize the rest by the user's decision sequence: condition, immediate results, area/category, then guides. Reject a search box floating in a giant slogan Hero, results presented as generic marketing cards, or brand copy pushing search below the fold.

## Hub 2 — Date and calendar axis

Choose this for theater, readings, performance, nightlife, or any portal whose primary decision is time. Use a horizontal date strip or time selector as the main navigation axis, directly connected to one featured event and the weekend or upcoming list. Time and place must organize the entire Page rather than appearing as tiny card badges. Continue from today to week, venues, and later dates while keeping the active window legible. Reject a decorative date strip, a featured event without time/place, or later sections reverting to ordinary category cards.

## Hub 3 — Featured-card mosaic

Choose this for routes, maker events, screenings, art, or trend discovery. Build the opening from one clearly dominant cross-column card and several smaller fragments; search may sit in the header or immediately above. The main card owns the most important current event and real photography, while fragments represent different categories, places, or times. Continue into trend masonry, creators/venues, and collections while maintaining size priority. Reject equal cards, a featured card without imagery, repeated copies of the same event, or empty stretched cards.

## Hub 4 — Practical directory cards

Choose this for studios, venues, wineries, yoga, healthcare, or professional-service directories. Search and region/category chips lead directly into actionable entries containing decision fields such as hours, area, service type, address, availability, or next event. Continue by region, service, or open status; trust and statistics come only after the directory has established utility. Reject a corporate marketing homepage, entries missing time/place/service fields, or filters that do not correspond to visible result information.

## Hub 5 — Search and interface bento

Choose this for tools, podcasts, automation, resources, digital art, or productized knowledge catalogs. Search is the entry, while bento regions represent real categories, shows, use cases, prices, snippets, or interface previews that lead into browseable collections. The Page may borrow interface richness from software products, but its information architecture remains a directory rather than a single-product landing page. Reject decorative dashboard cards, search without result previews, bento blocks with no real destination, and a fallback sequence of features, pricing, and generic CTA.
