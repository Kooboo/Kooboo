# Shop Page Composition Families

These eight families were distilled from 39 of the 88 reviewed template sites. Choose one primary family for a high-expression retail Page. Adapt it to the current UUPM blocks; never use it to change those blocks.

## Shop 1 — Full-bleed product stage

Choose this when one product or a small range has photography strong enough to carry the first viewport. The product image fills the viewport and the brand line plus real shopping action belongs to the same visual plane, usually near the lower image area rather than in a detached left copy column. Let the next category, use-case strip, or product row appear at the lower edge so the visitor understands where to shop after the image. Continue with category branches, representative products, and proof or use scenes. Reject a silent image wall, low-contrast overlay text, and a generic three-card second section.

## Shop 2 — Editorial product catalog

Choose this for fashion, footwear, boards, swim, or accessories where the range itself creates the brand. Put a strong newspaper-like masthead and the first products in the same viewport instead of building a separate marketing Hero. Product photography supplies the color; price, filters, and category labels behave like an editorial index. Use modest variation in crop or scale so the catalog has hierarchy. Reject a masthead that consumes the whole viewport, identical rounded product cards, repeated shadows, and a detached slogan section before products.

## Shop 3 — Lookbook masonry

Choose this when lifestyle scenes and purchasable items need to tell one story. Build the first one or two screens around an intentional image masonry with one genuinely dominant scene and smaller products or scenes arranged around it. Alternate lifestyle context with purchasable items through the Page instead of filling every row with equal SKU cards. Keep product labels quiet and spatially connect a scene with the relevant items. Reject random different-height cards without a visual anchor, a dominant image isolated from products, and masonry whose inner cards leave large empty areas.

## Shop 4 — Still-life boutique window

Choose this for fragrance, skincare, ceramics, candles, soap, homeware, or tactile premium goods. Use a centered or restrained masthead with composed still-life photography, deliberate whitespace, hairlines, and material detail. The first viewport still needs a category or selected-product path near its lower edge; atmosphere alone is insufficient. Let the Page move slowly through a small number of categories, selected products, craft/material narrative, and then trust or subscription. Reject generic beige journal styling, tiny images floating in excessive whitespace, and every section being centered with equal emphasis.

## Shop 5 — Maker's counter

Choose this for tools, desk objects, textiles, craft materials, stationery, or intimate handmade shops. The first viewport resembles a real counter or worktable with several related objects forming one horizontal still life. Category text or small labels sit close to the corresponding objects, creating an immediate browsing path. Later sections unpack material, purpose, and category before a denser product list. Reject a single background image covered by a long speech, equal category cards that erase the display-counter feeling, or a sudden oversized technology slogan.

## Shop 6 — Activity scene and collection rail

Choose this for outdoor, sport, snow, grilling, camping, or products whose use environment is central. Let action or environmental photography establish speed, weather, or place across the full width. A collection/equipment/technology rail must directly bridge that scene into products at the viewport edge or immediately below. Continue as an activity unfolds: scene, collection choice, core equipment, then technical or community content. Reject an atmospheric photograph followed by unrelated product cards, a split athlete-and-copy Hero, or SaaS feature cards used for equipment benefits.

## Shop 7 — DTC product narrative

Choose this for food, supplements, care, or packaged goods that require a concise persuasion sequence. Product packaging and a short benefit lead together; the visitor sees what is sold before mission language or media logos. Move quickly through benefits, flavor/variant, use moment, proof, and purchase. Keep one continuous argument rather than a collection of icon cards. Reject a small product pushed to a corner, every benefit rendered as an identical card, proof preceding product recognition, or abstract brand purpose taking over the first viewport.

## Shop 8 — Playful brand world

Choose this for youth care, stationery, pets, or expressive packaging. Use color blocks, arches, irregular edges, or generous rounded shapes to make a distinctive world, but every decorative shape must carry a product, category, or real action. Maintain one clear color hierarchy and let sections change shape or color while staying anchored to specific merchandise. Reject floating decoration with no task, emoji as the main source of playfulness, every block using the same radius, or visual effects that compress product information and shopping paths.
