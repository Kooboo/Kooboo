# Magazine Page Composition Families

These seven families were distilled from 20 of the 88 reviewed template sites. Choose one primary family for a publication or editorial discovery Page. Preserve the current UUPM blocks and content requirements.

## Magazine 1 — Hard-cropped issue cover

Choose this when the publication should feel like a current issue. A central masthead, issue/date/category details, and one decisively cropped cover photograph must read as one artifact. Buttons stay scarce, while the lower viewport reveals the beginning of the story index so the cover is not an isolated poster. Continue from current issue to story index, columned features, and archive. Reject a cover reduced to a small centered card, a giant meaningless gap between masthead and image, or an unrelated marketing CTA band.

## Magazine 2 — News river

Choose this for architecture criticism, public affairs, city issues, or text-rich long-form publishing. Enter the content river immediately: one lead story spans a row or multiple columns while supporting stories vary in width and text density. Hairlines and alignment create the reading path; floating shadow cards do not. Keep changing story priority and section density down the Page while preserving a clear lead anchor. Reject equal three-column blog cards, repeated section headings with no hierarchy, and every story receiving the same image and type scale.

## Magazine 3 — Dark cinematic masonry

Choose this for film, music, nightlife, or visual-culture criticism. A dark, restrained chrome lets stills and night photography become the light source. One major review or image spans the composition, surrounded by shorter reviews, ratings, schedules, or archive cues. Continue into themed masonry, rankings, or screening archives without adding a generic slogan Hero. Reject identical poster cards, neon controls competing with every image, low-contrast body copy, and decorative gradients that make the Page look like a software launch.

## Magazine 4 — Brutalist index

Choose this for type, design, craft, or subculture publishing. Strong rules, square edges, category filters, numbering, and a high-contrast masthead share the first viewport with the opening index or masonry. The identity comes from information organization and typography, not from soft photography or rounded cards. Keep the grid visible and occasionally let one large image interrupt the rigorous rhythm. Reject merely changing to a bold font while retaining a generic blog structure, or drawing so many lines that every item has equal importance.

## Magazine 5 — Journey-led staggered narrative

Choose this for travel, outdoors, gardens, or place-based culture. Begin with environmental photography that establishes place, then let the first stories intrude into or visually extend from its lower edge. Alternate full-width environments, narrow text passages, and staggered stories so the Page feels like a route unfolding. Reject a full-bleed photo followed by detached equal cards, identical image ratios for every destination, and generic feature sections unrelated to the opening place.

## Magazine 6 — Architectural type over image

Choose this for architecture, homes, spaces, or project-focused editorial. Oversized project typography and architecture photography must occupy the same visual plane, using sky, facade, or intentional negative space for overlap. Continue like a project feature: overview, central question or solution, detail slices, then method or people. Reject separating the large title into its own band to avoid overlap, turning the Page into a real-estate lead-generation site, or following the opening project with unrelated service cards.

## Magazine 7 — Experimental editorial collage

Choose this for creative culture, photography, body art, or highly branded editorial. Collage masks, scallops, portrait columns, glass controls, oversized rounded covers, or type passing through images are allowed only when one editorial anchor remains unmistakable: masthead, issue, lead story, or section. Let later collage, portrait, accordion, and story-mosaic sections vary while retaining one typography system. Reject a different visual language in every section, collage that obscures the lead headline, and spectacle without an identifiable reading entry.
